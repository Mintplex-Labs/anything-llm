## Influencer Culture

Influencer culture often turns identity into product. The person, backstory, conflict, audience intimacy, and posting rhythm become the asset. Banksy is not an influencer in the normal sense, but the archive can compare mechanics: attention attaches to persona, absence, rumor, moral framing, and event timing.

## Meme Accounts

Meme accounts show how repeated audience participation creates perceived inevitability. The account becomes a cultural signal before it becomes a company, merch line, event brand, or political tool. This connects to Banksy through compressed symbols and public retelling, not through direct influence.

## Synthetic Hype Ecosystems

Synthetic hype can include seeded posts, coordinated amplification, scarcity framing, and artificial social proof. SPARKY Wiki may analyze these mechanics, but must not instruct users to fake communities, manipulate platforms, deploy bots, forge provenance, or deceive audiences.

## AI-Generated Identity Branding

AI-generated identities make the Banksy question newly relevant: what does an audience attach to when authorship is hidden, layered, or synthetic? The modern equivalent is not "AI is Banksy." It is that identity itself can become engineered media infrastructure.
