# Banksy Criticism Layer

Status: depth-tree foundation marker preserved for depth-tree guards.

This layer surfaces criticism, commercialization concerns, authenticity disputes, market capture, institutional adoption, and counter-readings.

## Criticism Is Part Of The Intelligence

SPARKY Wiki should not treat Banksy as a one-way hero narrative. The archive is stronger when it can hold admiration and critique together. Criticism of Banksy often clusters around four tensions:

- anti-capitalist imagery versus high auction prices
- public-space critique versus property-owner cost
- anonymity/authenticity versus brand-scale recognition
- rebellion versus institutional absorption

These are not automatic refutations. They are pressure points. Sparky should use them to deepen the conversation rather than collapse into praise or dismissal.

## Anti-Commercial Versus Market Capture

Claim `banksy-market-002` gives a cited market anchor for _Love is in the Bin_. Claim `banksy-market-003` interprets the auction spectacle as part of the market narrative. Critics can argue that market value swallows critique: the more the work mocks the system, the more valuable the system makes it.

Counterargument: that contradiction may be part of the work's cultural force. A critique that gets absorbed by the market can still reveal how absorption works. Sparky should present both readings.

## Public Good Versus Private Cost

The _Slave Labour_ controversy (`banksy-source-conflict-001`) helps expose a criticism that public attention does not erase private/public cost. A work that becomes beloved in a community may still sit on someone else's wall. Removal may feel like theft from public memory; keeping it may create liability or crowd pressure for the property holder.

The criticism layer should not decide this too quickly. It should map who was affected.

## Regeneration Critique

Dismaland, Port Talbot, and Glasgow show how street-culture signals can be turned into cultural tourism, museum attendance, or civic story. That can be positive. It can also become commercialization of rebellion. The criticism is not "regeneration is bad"; it is that regeneration often sells authenticity after risk was created elsewhere.

## What Sparky Must Not Do

- Do not present criticism as verified motive.
- Do not present market absorption as proof of secret coordination.
- Do not erase property-owner or local-resident costs.
- Do not reduce political art to marketing mechanics only.
- Do not turn critique into operational illegal guidance.

## Useful Counterquestions

- Who benefits from this reading?
- Who pays the cost?
- Does the critique survive market absorption?
- Is this public memory, private property, or both?
- Does institutional adoption preserve the work or neutralize it?
