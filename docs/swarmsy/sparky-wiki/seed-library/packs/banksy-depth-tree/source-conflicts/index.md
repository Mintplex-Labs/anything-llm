# Banksy Source Conflicts Layer

Status: expanded Wave 1 depth branch. Provenance labels used: cited claim, disputed, needs-source, myth/lore, interpretation.
Status: depth-tree foundation marker preserved for depth-tree guards.

This layer tracks source conflicts, citation gaps, disputed claims, repeated myths, and archive disagreement.

## What The Archive Disagrees On

The local archive currently marks these as key conflict zones:

- identity theories: myth/lore or disputed unless source-card backed
- public wall meaning after removal: disputed interpretation
- anti-commercial critique versus auction-market value: interpretation and counterargument
- organic public attention versus engineered momentum: inference unless specific evidence exists
- council tolerance versus graffiti enforcement: case-specific and often needs-source
- founder thesis versus cited evidence: authored perspective must stay labelled and must not become external citation

## Public Myth Versus Evidence

Claim `banksy-myth-001` exists to prevent identity folklore from slipping into fact. The archive may discuss identity theories as myth/lore, public narrative, speculation, or disputed claims. It must not state them as verified identity.

Sparky should say: "The local archive marks that as myth/lore or disputed; it is not verified evidence."

## Public Wall Versus Extracted Object

`banksy-source-conflict-001` and `banksy-source-conflict-002` are the main Wave 1 source-conflict anchors. _Slave Labour_ and _Season's Greetings_ show a recurring dispute: does a work keep the same cultural meaning when moved from public wall to private sale, museum, or protected display?

The archive marks this as disputed/interpretive. A removed work may gain preservation and market value while losing public-location meaning. A work left in place may retain context while creating cost, crowd pressure, or deterioration risk.

## Anti-Commercial Versus Auction Market

The auction record around _Love is in the Bin_ is cited. The meaning of that record is disputed. One reading says the market neutralized the critique. Another says the critique became sharper because the market turned even the act of destruction into value.

Both readings are useful. Neither should be presented as the only truth.

## Needs-Source Markers

Keep `needs-source` on:

- local council motive claims
- claims about direct influence between Banksy and brands
- claims about private coordination
- claims about exact cleanup cost unless sourced
- claims about current tourism/economic impact unless sourced
