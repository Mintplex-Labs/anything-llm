# ARG Mystery Trail Protocol

## Purpose

Explain how mystery trails, clues, staged reveals, community solving, and archive breadcrumbs can build participatory attention.

## What It Explains

- ARG design
- mystery trail
- clue sequence
- community solving

## Confidence Model

- verified: ARGs and mystery campaigns leave public clues, communities, archives, and solving timelines.
- likely: a trail works when clues are fair, safe, paced, and worth archiving.
- inferred: mystery increases investment when participants feel agency rather than deception.
- speculative / avoid claiming: secret private plans, unsafe scavenger routes, or guaranteed engagement.

- synthetic social proof changes perception by making people feel a movement, object, or identity is already culturally validated.

- long-term trust/reputation consequences include authenticity collapse, platform penalties, collector distrust, community resentment, and brand fatigue.
- legal/ethical risk includes misleading sponsorship, fake scarcity, impersonation, undisclosed coordination, market manipulation, consumer deception, and platform-rule violations.
- what is verified vs inferred: use public records, campaign credits, disclosures, media artifacts, and observable timelines; label hidden coordination as inference unless sourced.
- safer/transparent alternatives: disclose sponsorship, use honest scarcity, build real community participation, publish provenance, and make the staged parts clear.
